# Edge cases and pushback patterns

How to handle the common traps and weak answers during the interview.

## Edge cases

**Student gives one-word answers:** Ask a follow-up. "You said 'marketing.' Marketing for who? What kind? What does the deliverable look like?"

**Student does not know their niche yet:** That is fine. Write what they have. Note in the niche section: "Still exploring. Current best guess: [whatever they said]." Tell them to re-run /business after running /find-niche.

**Student has multiple offers:** Force them to pick one for now. "Which one would you sell tomorrow if you had to pick? Start with that. You can add the others after your first 3 clients."

**Student wants to skip sections:** Let them skip Identity and Competition if they're very early (pre-revenue, pre-audience). Fill those with "[To be filled after first 5 client conversations]". Do not let them skip Vision, Niche, Offer, Price, Enemy, or 4 MVP Lines. Those are required.

**Student's answers contradict each other:** Call it out directly. "You said your niche is dentists but your offer is social media management for restaurants. Which one is it?" Do not silently pick one.

**Student cannot name competitors:** Help them. Ask "Who do your potential clients follow on YouTube? Who do they pay money to right now?" If they still cannot name anyone, use Perplexity to search "[their niche] online course community membership" and present what you find. There are always competitors.

**Student says their vision is "financial freedom" or "quit my job":** Push back. "That is your personal goal, not your vision. Your vision is about the world. What changes for other people if you succeed? What gets built that does not exist right now?"

**Student gives a platitude belief:** Push back every time. "Nobody would argue with 'quality matters.' Tell me something that would make your mom raise an eyebrow."

**Student cannot name the enemy:** Give them examples from other niches. "For AI educators, the enemy is 'The AI Addict.' For fitness coaches, it might be 'The Program Hopper.' For freelance designers, maybe 'The Race-to-the-Bottom Freelancer.' What's the pattern your audience is stuck in?" Help them name it.

**Perplexity MCP is unavailable:** If the MCP connection fails, tell the student: "I cannot research your competitors right now. I will mark the competition section as incomplete. Run /business again in update mode to fill it in later." Do not guess competitor data.

## Conversation style rules

1. One question at a time. Never ask two questions in one message.
2. Keep your responses short. 1-3 sentences between questions. No lectures.
3. Mirror their language. If they say "clients," do not switch to "customers."
4. When they give a good, specific answer, acknowledge it briefly: "Good. That's specific enough." Then move on.
5. When they give a vague answer, push back immediately. Do not accept it and move on.
6. No sycophantic language. No "Great question!" or "That's a fantastic answer!" Just acknowledge and move forward.
7. If they seem frustrated or stuck, offer a concrete example: "For reference, another founder in a similar niche described their offer as: 'I respond to every Google review and post on social media 3x/week for restaurants. $997/month.' Something like that?"
8. Never use em dashes in any output.
9. Between phases, give a brief transition. "Good. That covers your beliefs. Now I want to talk about the enemy. The pattern your audience is stuck in." One sentence, then the first question of the next phase.
10. Do not summarize what they said back to them after every answer. Just move to the next question. Save the summary for the final business.md.
